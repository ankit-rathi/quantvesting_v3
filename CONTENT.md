Absolutely. In fact, I think this is **one of the strongest ways to build the Quantvesting content ecosystem**.

Instead of creating a Reel and a Sketch Note as two separate pieces, make them **two views of the same idea**:

> **Sketch Note = the visual memory**
> **Reel/Short = the visual story**
> **Journal/Playbook = the deeper explanation**

And most importantly, the episodes should feel like **one continuous story**, where today's concept creates the question that tomorrow's concept answers.

I would structure the series as a **60–90 day “Quantvesting Visual Learning Journey.”**

### The storytelling rule

Every row should answer:

**What problem did we just discover → what question does it create → what concept answers that question → what new question does that create?**

That gives you a natural narrative instead of 60 unrelated finance videos.

# The 60–90 second storytelling formula

I'd make this almost mechanical.

### 0–5 sec — HOOK

Don't start with:

> “Today we're going to talk about Conviction.”

Start with a problem:

> **“Would you buy a stock just because it is ranked #12?”**

Immediately show **Rank #12** on the sketch.

---

### 5–20 sec — CREATE THE PROBLEM

Draw:

```text
Rank #12
     ↓
      ?
```

Then say:

> “Rank tells us where a company sits in the framework. But it doesn't tell us everything about how the framework sees that company.”

Now the audience has a question.

---

### 20–45 sec — BUILD THE CONCEPT

Start adding to the sketch:

```text
Rank
  +
Conviction
  +
Market Cap
```

Explain each visually.

Don't show the finished diagram at once.

**Build it while speaking.**

---

### 45–65 sec — THE AHA MOMENT

Now reveal the complete visual.

For example:

```text
X-LC
│ │
│ └── Large Cap
└──── Excellent
```

Then:

> “So X-LC isn't simply saying ‘this is a good company’. It is combining the framework's conviction with the company's market-cap context.”

That's your **aha moment**.

---

### 65–80 sec — WHY IT MATTERS

Connect it to the investor:

> “And that's important because two companies with similar ranks can still represent very different portfolio questions.”

Now you're naturally setting up the next episode.

---

### 80–90 sec — NEXT QUESTION

Don't end with:

> “Follow for more finance content.”

Instead:

> **“But conviction alone still doesn't tell us how much room might remain. That's where FTT comes in.”**

**Cut.**

Tomorrow starts with:

> “So what exactly is FTT?”

That is how you create a **series people can follow**.

# The most important visual rule

I'd actually give you one strict rule:

> **Never explain a concept that you cannot draw.**

If the concept is:

**Remaining Upside**

don't write:

> Remaining Upside = (FTT − Current Price) / Current Price

and spend 60 seconds explaining mathematics.

Instead draw:

```text
CURRENT PRICE              FTT
     ●──────────────────────●
            ↑
       remaining room
```

Then animate the distance.

The audience **sees the concept before they hear the definition**.

That's much closer to the “visual data storyteller” identity you're aiming for.
