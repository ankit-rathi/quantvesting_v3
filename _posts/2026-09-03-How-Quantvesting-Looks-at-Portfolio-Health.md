When investors look at a portfolio, the first number they often notice is the return.

That number matters. But it is only one part of the picture.

A portfolio can be profitable and still be poorly structured. It can also have a disappointing period while remaining reasonably constructed. This is why Quantvesting treats **Portfolio Health** as a broader question than performance alone.

### Start with what you actually own

The first job is to turn a list of holdings into a portfolio view.

Quantvesting brings together information such as:

- current portfolio value,
- capital deployed,
- number of holdings,
- target value and target profit where available,
- allocation across holdings,
- concentration, and
- the role or type of individual holdings within the framework.

The purpose is not to create a magic score. It is to make the portfolio's structure visible enough that an investor can ask better questions.

### Allocation changes the meaning of a holding

Owning a good company is not the same thing as having an appropriate position size.

A holding that represents a small part of a portfolio has a different portfolio impact from the same holding at a much larger weight. Concentration can also emerge gradually, especially when a successful holding appreciates faster than the rest of the portfolio.

That is why allocation deserves to be looked at alongside the individual security.

Quantvesting's configured portfolio rules include a standard maximum allocation of 3%, an exceptional maximum of 5%, and a sector maximum of 20%. These are framework thresholds to help identify situations worth examining; they are not automatic trading instructions.

### Health is a starting point, not a verdict

A portfolio-health view should answer:

**Where should I look more closely?**

It should not pretend to answer every investment question immediately.

If the portfolio shows an unusual concentration, a holding reaching a target, or an area that deserves more attention, the next step is investigation. The framework is useful when it reduces the amount of searching required to find those areas.

### Why this matters

Without a structured portfolio view, investors can spend time reacting to whichever stock or headline happens to be most visible.

With a structured view, the order becomes calmer:

**See the structure → identify what deserves attention → investigate the evidence.**

That is the role of Portfolio Health in Quantvesting. It is not a final answer. It is the map that helps decide where to look next.
