One of the most useful questions an investor can ask is not **"What should I buy?"**

It is:

> **What in my existing portfolio deserves another look?**

That is the purpose of **Review Areas** in Quantvesting.

### A review flag is deliberately not a trading instruction

Quantvesting uses review language rather than buy and sell language because a framework can identify a situation without having enough context to make a personal decision for an investor.

A review flag means: **look here next**.

The investigation might confirm that nothing needs to change. It might also uncover a reason to reconsider the position. The important thing is that the decision follows the investigation rather than replacing it.

### What can create a review situation?

The framework can surface different kinds of situations, including holdings where a target has been reached, legacy holdings that deserve examination, or situations where another opportunity creates a reason to compare alternatives.

Quantvesting's rotation configuration also uses thesis-captured thresholds. The current framework defines watch, review, strong-review and target levels, together with a minimum alternative-upside threshold of 20%.

These thresholds help structure attention. They do not automatically tell the investor what action to take.

### Review means bringing evidence back into the conversation

Suppose a holding has reached an important target.

The useful response is not automatically to sell it.

Instead, ask:

- What was the original thesis?
- What has changed since the position was established?
- Has the thesis already been captured in the current price?
- Is there a stronger alternative worth researching?
- Would changing the position improve the portfolio, or simply create another decision?

This is where qualitative judgment remains important.

### Why review areas are valuable

A portfolio can contain dozens of holdings. Not all of them deserve equal research time every day.

Review Areas create a prioritisation layer between raw portfolio data and human judgment.

The sequence becomes:

**Portfolio → Review Area → Evidence → Investigation → Decision**

Sometimes that sequence ends with **do nothing**.

That is not a failure of the framework. It is one of the useful outcomes of having a disciplined review process: the investor can decide not to act because the evidence does not justify action.

Quantvesting is therefore not trying to eliminate judgment. It is trying to help judgment start in the right place.
