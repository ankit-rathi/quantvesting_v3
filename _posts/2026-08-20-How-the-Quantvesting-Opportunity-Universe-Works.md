A portfolio assessment tells you what you already own.

But an investor also needs a way to ask a different question:

> **What is the framework currently finding interesting outside my existing holdings?**

That is the role of the **Opportunity Universe**.

### It begins with a defined research universe

Quantvesting does not start by scanning every security and then picking whichever names look exciting.

The framework has configured inclusion and exclusion rules, conviction categories, and a ranking system. The current ranking framework groups measures into four broad categories:

- **Value** — measures such as deviation from the 200-day average, valuation measures and ROE relative to PE.
- **Growth** — sales growth and profit growth.
- **Quality** — measures including FTT%, market capitalisation, ROE and cash-flow conversion.
- **Momentum** — measures including recent gains and RSI.

The categories currently carry equal weights in the configuration.

### Ranking is a way to organise research

A ranking does not turn uncertainty into certainty.

It does something more practical: it gives the investor an ordered starting point for further work.

The configured ranking directions differ by metric. For example, some value measures favour lower readings while growth and quality measures generally favour higher readings. The framework combines these inputs rather than relying on a single indicator.

This matters because an attractive-looking stock on one dimension can look very different when considered across value, growth, quality and momentum together.

### The Opportunity Universe is deliberately filtered

In the current Web assessment, the Opportunity Universe is built from the **top 40 ranked securities** and then filtered to securities with **Upside % greater than 20%**.

That is a research-view construction, not a promise that every displayed security is suitable for every investor.

The purpose is to narrow a large research problem into a manageable list of candidates worth investigating.

### What should happen after a candidate appears?

The candidate is not the conclusion.

The next step is to understand why it ranks well and then investigate the underlying business, valuation, growth, quality and momentum evidence. Qualitative analysis still matters because a rules-based ranking cannot capture every fact that may influence a decision.

This is why Quantvesting treats the Opportunity Universe as:

**Rank → Filter → Investigate**

rather than:

**Rank → Buy**

### Why this component exists

A good portfolio framework should help with both sides of the decision process:

**What deserves review in what I already own?**

and

**Where might further research be worthwhile?**

The Opportunity Universe is the second question. Its job is not to remove uncertainty. Its job is to make the next research step clearer.
